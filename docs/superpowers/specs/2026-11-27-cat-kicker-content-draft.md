## The project

Two fabric rectangles, sewn right sides together along both long edges to make a tube, turned right side out, stuffed (stuffing or scrap fabric) and closed at both ends. Optional catnip. Cut size 15 × 40 cm, 1 cm seam allowance, about 13 × 38 cm finished.

## Section 1: Set up your machine

1. **Read your machine manual.** Find the pages on threading, the bobbin and stitch selection. *Why:* every machine threads slightly differently, and the manual is the only source that is right for yours. (`sewing-machine-manual`)
2. **Wind a bobbin.** Wind thread onto an empty bobbin. *Why:* the bobbin supplies the lower thread; without it no stitch can form. (`winding-a-bobbin`)
3. **Thread the machine.** Thread the top, insert the bobbin and bring the bottom thread up. *Why:* a stitch is two threads locking together, and wrong threading is the top cause of tangles and skipped stitches. (`threading-your-machine`)
4. **Choose a straight stitch.** Set stitch length about 2.5 mm and the needle in the centre. *Why:* a medium stitch is strong enough for a toy and still easy to unpick. (`straight-stitch-and-seam-allowance`)
5. **Test on a scrap.** Sew a short line on two scraps of your fabric. *Why:* testing shows tension and threading problems before they ruin your real pieces. (`straight-stitch-and-seam-allowance`, `seam-ripper`)

## Section 2: Gather your tools and materials

6. **Lay out your tools.** Tick every tool in the checklist above. *Why:* hunting for scissors mid-seam is how fabric gets pulled out of line. (`cutting-tools`, `fabric-marking-tools`, `rulers-and-measuring-tape`, `pins-and-clips`, `seam-ripper`)
7. **Lay out your materials.** Fabric, thread, stuffing or scraps, and catnip if using. *Why:* checking quantities now avoids running short halfway through. (`choosing-fabric`, `stuffing-and-fillings`)

## Section 3: Prepare your fabric and cut

8. **Choose your fabric.** Pick a tightly woven cotton or similar. *Why:* woven fabric does not stretch while you sew, so it is easiest for a first project, and tight weaves keep stuffing in. (`fabric-woven-vs-knit`, `choosing-fabric`)
9. **Wash and dry the fabric.** *Why:* fabric can shrink the first time it is washed, and you want that to happen before the toy exists. (`preparing-fabric`)
10. **Press the fabric flat.** *Why:* creases change your measurements, so pieces cut from wrinkled fabric come out the wrong size. (`iron-and-ironing-board`, `pressing-seams`)
11. **Mark two rectangles, 15 × 40 cm.** Use a ruler and marking tool. *Why:* marked lines are straighter than guessing by eye, and both pieces must match for the seam to line up. (`fabric-marking-tools`, `rulers-and-measuring-tape`, `measuring-and-cutting`)
12. **Cut out both rectangles.** *Why:* clean, straight cuts keep the seam allowance even all the way along. (`cutting-tools`, `cutting-blades`, `cutting-mat`)
13. **Cut your scrap filling (if using scraps).** Cut clean scraps into pieces smaller than about 3 cm. *Why:* small pieces fill corners evenly and don't make lumps. (`stuffing-and-fillings`)

## Section 4: Sew the toy

14. **Pin right sides together.** Place the pretty sides facing, edges matching, and pin along both long sides. *Why:* seams hide on the inside, and pins stop the layers shifting under the needle. (`pins-and-clips`)
15. **Sew the first long side.** 1 cm seam allowance, backstitch at both ends. *Why:* backstitching locks the thread so seams don't unravel when the toy is kicked. (`straight-stitch-and-seam-allowance`, `backstitching`)
16. **Sew the second long side.** *Why:* two parallel seams make a tube; the open ends are how you will turn and stuff it. (`straight-stitch-and-seam-allowance`, `backstitching`, `seam-ripper`)
17. **Press the seams open.** *Why:* flat seams make the tube turn smoothly and the finished toy look neat. (`pressing-seams`, `iron-and-ironing-board`)
18. **Turn right side out.** Push the tube through itself and use a blunt tool for the corners. *Why:* this puts your neat seams inside and the pretty fabric outside. (`turning-and-stuffing`)
19. **Stuff firmly.** Push stuffing or scraps in to the ends. Add a pinch of catnip in the middle if using. *Why:* a firm toy gives a cat something to grip and kick; a loose one flops. (`stuffing-and-fillings`, `turning-and-stuffing`)
20. **Fold in the raw ends and pin.** Fold each open end in by 1 cm, edges together. *Why:* hiding the raw edges stops fraying and gives a clean finish. (`pins-and-clips`)
21. **Close both ends.** Sew across each end close to the edge with a straight stitch (or by hand with ladder stitch), backstitching at both sides. *Why:* closed ends hold the stuffing in, which matters for your cat's safety. (`ladder-stitch`, `backstitching`, `straight-stitch-and-seam-allowance`)
22. **Check and finish.** Tug every seam, trim loose threads, check nothing small can come off. *Why:* cats chew and kick hard, and a weak seam or loose trim is a hazard, so supervise play at first. (`cat-toy-safety`)
